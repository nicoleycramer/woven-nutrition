## History

The concept of intuitive eating originated in the 1970s through registered dietitian Thelma Wayler's non-diet program and psychotherapist Susie Orbach's exploration of weight's social construction, including her 1978 publication "Fat is a Feminist Issue." Author Geneen Roth continued developing related ideas throughout the 1980s. The term "intuitive eating" itself was established and popularized by registered dietitians Evelyn Tribole and Elyse Resch in 1995, describing "a self-care eating framework that encompasses 10 principles." By 2022, over 100 research studies examined intuitive eating and comparable non-diet frameworks.

## Principles of Intuitive Eating

The framework represents "a dynamic interplay between thought, emotion, and instinct, which together help us build awareness and connection with our body's sensations to support our well-being." Importantly, intuitive eating functions as an ongoing practice rather than a linear process, requiring patience, introspection, and self-compassion.

### 1. Reject the Diet Mentality

Diet culture perpetuates the false message that thinness equals health. This messaging comes from social media, press, healthcare professionals, family, and friends. The $70+ billion diet industry normalizes dieting and weight loss pursuit so thoroughly that recognizing subtle diet culture subscriptions proves challenging. The first principle demands rejecting diet culture's grip through awareness — noticing and naming it when encountered.

### 2. Honor Your Hunger

"Hunger is one of our basic biological needs. It's our body's way of telling us that it needs energy and nutrition." Building trust with internal hunger signals through consistent, adequate nourishment parallels responding naturally to bathroom urges. Disrupted hunger attunement sometimes requires registered dietitian support for identification and resolution.

### 3. Make Peace with Food

Food moralization — labeling items "good" versus "bad," "healthy" versus "unhealthy," "clean" versus "dirty" — creates guilt, shame, deprivation, and loss of control. Viewing all foods neutrally through unconditional permission supports peaceful food relationships.

### 4. Challenge the Food Police

Diet culture develops internal "food police" — beliefs and rules about eating. Overcoming this involves mindful awareness of food thoughts, curious reflection, and reframing through neutral or positive statements.

### 5. Discover the Satisfaction Factor

Food transcends nutrition, encompassing "connection, joy, pleasure, fellowship, culture." Meals satisfying physiological hunger may leave emotional hunger unmet. Creating inviting eating environments and allowing cravings support satisfaction discovery.

### 6. Feel Your Fullness

Honoring fullness signals mirrors honoring hunger cues. Pausing during meals to assess comfort levels, hunger status, and cravings builds body trust. Consistent signal responsiveness creates safety beneficial for mental and physical wellbeing.

### 7. Cope with Your Emotions with Kindness

Food provides temporary comfort, though diversified coping mechanisms better serve emotional needs. When turning toward food emotionally, assess actual needs — solitude, friendship, walking, breathing exercises, or journaling might address underlying issues more effectively than eating alone.

### 8. Respect Your Body

Bodies naturally vary in shape and size, reflecting genetic blueprints creating individual uniqueness. While "body love" feels unrealistic for many, achieving body neutrality represents an accessible alternative. Body respect improves self-perception and life engagement.

### 9. Movement – Feel the Difference

Rejecting diet culture's "all or nothing" thinking extends to movement. Rather than tracking external metrics like calories burned, focusing on how movement feels — energized, connected, proud of capability — honors personal wellness.

### 10. Honor Your Health with Gentle Nutrition

Ensuring adequate, regular food consumption represents the primary nutrition consideration. Choosing satisfying, nourishing foods supports health without demanding perfection.

## Associated Health Benefits

Research indicates intuitive eating correlates with "positive body image, improved self esteem, greater satisfaction with life, decreased disordered eating behaviors, and overall mental wellbeing." Physical benefits may include lower triglyceride levels.

## How Can I Start?

Recognizing that life circumstances affect principle accessibility, intuitive eating needn't be all-or-nothing. Individual experiences, personalities, goals, and conditions shape implementation approaches. Starting with resonating principles proves sufficient, with professional guidance available through consultation.
